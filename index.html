Philippians 4:13

Setting

Paul wrote this from prison, probably in Rome around AD 60–62, to thank the church at Philippi in Greece for a gift (4:10, 18). Roman prisoners usually relied on friends for food, so hunger was a real threat and their gift mattered.

Surrounding passage

Just before, Paul says he has learned to be content with plenty or with hunger (4:11–12). Just after, he thanks them for sharing his trouble and promises God will supply all they need (4:14, 19).

Key words

πάνταpanta · Greek

In context, the situations Paul has just named: plenty and hunger, having much and having little.

ἐνδυναμοῦντίendynamounti · Greek

The strength is Christ’s, not Paul’s own, and it keeps coming: enough to be content in any situation.

Commonly misused

Used as a slogan for winning games or reaching any goal you set.

Using it rightly

This is what Paul learned: Christ gives strength to be content whether full or hungry, rich or poor. That is what his “all” covers. Lean on it in hardship and in plenty alike, not as a promise you’ll win whatever you attempt.

Why the name Potentate?

It comes from 1 Timothy 6:15, where the KJV calls God “the blessed and only Potentate, the King of kings, and Lord of lords.”
